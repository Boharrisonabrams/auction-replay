Leading direction

Calculate value relative to the actual league: when paying above a reference price buys more useful starting-lineup production than waiting for the alternatives this room is likely to leave. Use plausible complete rosters to explain that calculation and the assumptions that could reverse it.

Codebase Context

The repository contains six workflow setup files, the concept, and a follow-up analysis of all 196 selections in Bo’s 2025 Yahoo league. Historical website access is verified; an application, live feed, and licensed product data source are not yet established. Bo’s experience is the starting evidence: waiting for bargains can leave him paying more for weaker remaining options. Yahoo and friend account access are part of his desired product; ESPN and monetization are future opportunities.

The competitive gap needs precision. Draft Sharks and FantasyPros already describe dynamic values influenced by rosters, budgets, scarcity, and league rules. Draft Sharks also advises against treating its values as rigid limits. Better dynamic pricing alone is an insufficient differentiation claim. Explicit roster alternatives and history-based manager estimates are hypotheses to test. Draft Intel’s exclusion of salary-cap drafts is a documented opening in one feature.

Yahoo access is a dependency to verify. Its current application process involves review and presently describes read-only access. The API documentation covers user authorization, league settings, teams, players, and draft results. Neither establishes this project’s access to real-time nominations, current bids, losing bids, refresh latency, or complete historical price fields. Older references to write scopes do not establish current write availability.

Data supply must support the intended use. FantasyPros’ public terms and current access guidance restrict competing uses. Its data licensing page describes separate commercial arrangements; no agreement for this product has been obtained. A public API key or paid consumer subscription should not be assumed to cover this assistant. Player projections and historical inputs need a permitted source before they become a product dependency.

ESPN is an open research path. The community espn-api project demonstrates access using league identifiers and, for private leagues, session-cookie values. It is not an ESPN-supported commercial integration agreement. That path does not yet establish suitable friend onboarding or redistribution rights.

Evidence limits. This is public-document research, not account access or a live auction test. Yahoo findings include current search-index excerpts because direct page retrieval was rate-limited. Vendor integration descriptions are claims to verify in a real draft. The cited optimization research concerns fantasy soccer or simplified auction models; it establishes useful prior art, not NFL win improvement.

Follow-up evidence: Bo’s clarification makes league-relative valuation the central product hypothesis. The 2025 draft-and-production analysis now matches every selection and separates keeper salaries from auction prices. His four WR purchases totaling $53 produced 308.40 season points; Puka Nacua cost $49 and produced 310.50. These historical outcomes supply a concrete benchmark, while draft-time advantage still requires testing.

Open inputs: upcoming draft date and venue, original tool values and projections, weekly lineup evidence, confirmed manager continuity, acceptable manual input, source permissions, and a measurable first-version success criterion.

1. See the roster you buy by paying or passing

Axis: Roster decisions

Confidence: 85%

Complexity: High

Description
For a nominated player, compare the complete legal rosters plausibly reachable if you win at the next price and if you pass. Show the premium breakpoint, the remaining budget reserve, and the performance or later-price assumptions that would reverse the advice. A premium is earned by the roster comparison; it can be zero when a balanced alternative is better.
Basis

direct: Bo described “always waiting for relative value” and then paying more for weaker players.

external: Draft Sharks already treats values as a baseline. A 2022 optimization paper demonstrates adaptive, budget-constrained roster optimization in fantasy soccer.

reasoned: A premium is justified only if its plausible complete roster beats the alternatives under explicit assumptions. This may favor stars or a balanced roster; it does not validate a fixed superstar markup.

Rationale
A concrete alternative roster and an explicit assumption explain when paying above a market reference is worthwhile.
Downsides
Useful advice depends on plausible future prices, player performance estimates, and league rules. Exact optimization is not the hard part by itself: a precise answer built on poor assumptions can still mislead. Established tools already adjust for roster needs, so the comparative experience must earn its value.

2. Estimate the room, with manager detail supported by evidence

Axis: Room behavior

Confidence: 75%

Complexity: High

Description
Start from a league-level range and the legal bids each opponent can still afford. Add manager-specific spending-plan and price patterns only where confirmed identities and comparable history support them, otherwise retain the broader estimate. Show likely room price separately from the roster-based bid ceiling; neither winning prices nor display names reveal hidden maximum bids or cross-league identity.
Basis

direct: Bo wants to use managers’ historical data “in this league and other leagues.”

external: FantasyPros Draft Intel explicitly excludes salary-cap drafts. That is a gap in this feature, not proof that no competing auction product models managers.

reasoned: Remaining budget and required roster spending constrain legal bids. Comparable histories may narrow price ranges, but winning sales do not reveal opponents’ hidden maximum bids, and display names do not establish identity.

Rationale
It addresses the strongest documented competitor gap without pretending small histories can read an opponent's mind.
Downsides
A few seasons provide sparse, changing observations. Sale prices do not reveal losing bidders’ limits. Cross-league identity needs confirmation, and personal history may add nothing beyond the league baseline. Forecasts must be judged on drafts withheld from model fitting.

3. Rehearse the expensive decision and keep its evidence

Axis: Shared access and trust

Confidence: 85%

Complexity: Medium

Description
Practice the premium-versus-pass moment under several plausible rooms, hiding later outcomes until the user chooses. Preserve the inputs, alternatives, recommendation, and user choice, then compare against static values, inflation-only guidance, and a basic constrained optimizer. Use actual permitted historical inputs when available and clearly labeled synthetic scenarios otherwise; fixed-price replay diagnoses choices but does not prove causal win gains. Include rooms where several friends use similar advice, because adoption itself changes demand.
Basis

direct: The motivating experience is missing elite players and later overpaying; actual league history has not been supplied.

external: The 2022 optimization paper uses out-of-sample simulated evaluation in fantasy soccer. A 2016 auction study examines auction strategies under simplified adversarial assumptions.

reasoned: Compare legal roster completion, projected roster value, price-range calibration, stranded budget, and decision time against baselines. Freeze information to what was available then. Test normal rooms as well as the reported trap, and vary opponents’ reactions.

Rationale
It can show whether users understand and follow the proposed advice and expose weak assumptions before the first live draft.
Downsides
Synthetic rooms can favor the algorithm that designed them; fixed historical prices cannot show what your different bids would have caused. Relevant historical inputs must have been available at the time. Draft quality and usability are measurable sooner than an effect on season wins.

4. A bid card that survives a lost connection

Axis: Live usability

Confidence: 80%

Complexity: Medium

Description
Borrow an aviation checklist: calculate a compact conditional bid card from the last confirmed auction state, so you can act during a fast bidding sequence without waiting for every price to sync. The card states the current nominee, bid ceiling, the roster concession at each material price breakpoint, and the events that invalidate it. When another sale or a correction changes the underlying state, suspend the card until you confirm that event through sync or quick manual entry. Record a confirmed sale and support undo; expose the exact state and conditions on which advice depends.
Basis

external: Yahoo’s current application page describes read-only access. Its API reference does not establish live nomination or bid-event coverage and latency for this project.

reasoned: An explicit conditional bid limit can remain useful during a bidding sequence without ingesting every price increment. It becomes invalid when an underlying assumption changes. Manual confirmed sale entry and undo provide a path to recover the state.

Rationale
You can test an actionable Yahoo assistant before a proven live bid feed exists, and you can tell exactly when its advice becomes stale.
Downsides
Manual sale entry adds work during a timed auction. A lost connection is tolerable only while the underlying state remains confirmed; if a sale is missing, suspend advice until it is corrected. This does not promise Yahoo reconciliation or future ESPN compatibility.

5. Private coaching for connected friends

Axis: Shared access and trust

Confidence: 80%

Complexity: Medium

Description
Each friend has an account and authorizes access to their own Yahoo leagues. Keep player beliefs, target rosters, bid limits, and recommendation history private, including between opponents in the same league. Reuse or share league facts only where source permissions and the affected users’ authorization allow it; connection alone does not grant permission to pool history. Personal disagreements with a baseline can change advice without exposing them to rivals.
Basis

direct: Bo wants “login management” so friends can connect their own leagues.

external: Yahoo documents user-authorized league access; FantasyPros documents restrictions on competing data use.

reasoned: Two connected friends can be auction opponents. Account identity, league access, data-sharing consent, and private strategy are separate concerns. Their connection alone authorizes no pooled intelligence or publication.

Rationale
Friend access is part of the requested usable product. Independent coaching makes that sharing useful even when friends compete directly, while allowing each user to challenge the same baseline.
Downsides
Private account boundaries need care from the first shared version. Identical advice can concentrate demand, so private strategy does not by itself prevent several users chasing the same players. Data redistribution and paid use remain unresolved.

6. Nominate to answer the next expensive question

Axis: Room behavior

Confidence: 60%

Complexity: Medium

Description
Recommend a nomination that distinguishes between two viable roster plans before you commit most of your budget. If your premium-quarterback plan depends on cheap receivers, for example, nominate a representative receiver whose actual sale would reveal whether that plan remains plausible, while accounting for the risk that you win the opening bid. The value of the nomination is the decision it clarifies, rather than a blanket instruction to drain opponents' money.
Basis

reasoned: If a star-heavy roster depends on buying cheap receivers later, observing a relevant receiver sale now may change that decision while alternatives remain. Nominate only a player whose acquisition is acceptable, and assess both possible outcomes. The nomination changes the room; it is not a controlled measurement of hidden demand.

Rationale
You can discover that a budget plan is unrealistic while you still have money and alternatives to change it.
Downsides
You can win the player you nominate, and a single sale has several possible causes. Nomination tactics already exist in competing tools. Information value is an experiment to validate after the core bid decision works, not evidence that an opponent’s hidden willingness to pay has been identified.

Rejection Summary

The 33 entries below were merged into stronger directions, deferred, or cut. Six candidates remain as the ranked ideas above. Independent basis review challenged every candidate; the shared-account idea was tightened to require permission, and automatic pooled intelligence was removed. All four axes retain survivors.
